Investigation.

TEST Forests are incredibly important ecosystems. They help regulate the climate, store carbon, support biodiversity, and maintain natural water cycles. Through my research, I discovered that climate change has increased the frequency and severity of droughts, which significantly raises the risk of forest fires. One of the key factors influencing wildfire risk is soil moisture. When soil and vegetation become dry, fires can ignite more easily and spread much faster, causing serious damage to forests, wildlife habitats, and nearby communities. While researching forest monitoring and fire prevention systems, I found that many rely on sensor networks to collect environmental data such as soil moisture, temperature, and rainfall. These systems are often part of Internet of Things (IoT) technologies, which enable real-time monitoring and early fire warnings. I also explored automated irrigation systems used in agriculture and greenhouses. These systems reduce water waste by activating only when soil moisture drops below a certain level. Learning about these technologies helped shape the design of my own project. Based on this research, I decided to investigate the relationship between water usage and forest fire risk by focusing on soil moisture levels. I chose soil moisture because it is a reliable indicator of drought conditions and can be easily measured using the analogue moisture sensor included in my greenhouse kit. To simulate drought conditions similar to those experienced in forests during extended dry periods, I restricted the amount of water available to the system. This allowed me to observe how reduced water supply leads to lower soil moisture and an increased simulated fire risk. I also built an adaptive response into my system. When soil moisture drops below a predefined safe level, a water pump automatically activates to simulate rainfall or irrigation. However, if water usage is limited and soil moisture remains low, the system signals a high fire risk through visual alerts. This mirrors how real-world forest fire warning systems respond to dangerous environmental conditions. Overall, my research reinforced the importance of effective water management in reducing wildfire risk. By modelling drought conditions and water usage in a small-scale greenhouse system, my project demonstrates how embedded systems and computer-based models can be used to monitor environmental changes, anticipate wildfire risk, and support more informed decision-making in forest and climate management.
