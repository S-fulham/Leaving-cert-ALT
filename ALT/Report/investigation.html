Investigation.

For my coursework, I researched many types of forest disasters such as flooding, fire and diesease. I started by making a list of all possible forest disaster scenarios on a mind map.


Existing disaster/forest monitoring systems:

Systems Sensors How it Works Advantages Disadvantages
Silvanet Wildfire Detection System (Dryad) Gas sensors (detect carbon monoxide, hydrogen, hydrocarbons)
Environmental sensors (temperature, humidity)		 Small sensors are attached to trees throughout the forest. They detect gases produced in the early stages of a fire. If abnormal gases are detected, the system sends an alert to the cloud and emergency services. Detects fires very early before flames spread. Sensors are small and low-cost. AI improves detection accuracy over time Requires many sensors to cover large forests. Sensors need power and maintenance
Smart Forest Flood Monitoring System (River Level Monitoring) Water level sensors Rainfall sensors Soil moisture sensors Sensors are placed near rivers and forest soil. They measure rainfall, soil moisture, and water levels. If levels rise too high, the system sends warnings to authorities to prepare for flooding. Helps predict floods before they occur Protects forest ecosystems and nearby communities Provides continuous environmental data Sensors can be damaged by extreme weather Requires power and internet connectivity
Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation (GEDI) – Satellite Forest Monitoring System LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) laser sensor The GEDI system is mounted on the International Space Station. It sends laser pulses toward Earth and measures the light that reflects back from trees. This allows scientists to measure forest height, biomass, and forest structure, helping monitor deforestation and carbon storage. Can measure forests across the entire planet Produces very detailed 3D maps of forest structure Helps scientists understand deforestation and carbon storage Data must be processed by scientists before use Satellites are expensive to build and launch

Dangers to Forests:

I researched the main dangers sourcses to forests and and found these to be the main factors:
  • Over 25,000 hectares of ash forests are estimated to be affected by ash dieback in the Republic of Ireland, as of April 2024.
  • Following Storm Éowyn in early 2025, over 26,000 hectares of forestry were reported as damaged or destroyed.
  • In 2023 wildfires destroyed 5,000 hectares of land.
  • Based on data from 2015–2020, the official rate of deforestation in Ireland (permanent removal of forest for other land use) was approximately 620 hectares per year.
I also researched nearby forest locations and found that the nearest forest to me is in an area known as Buffanoky between Cappamore and Doon, and over 5000 hectares were damaged in a fire in 2021.

Evaluation

From my research, I’ve found that:
- Most fire risk systems focus on Temperature, Humidity and Soil Moisture sensors.
- Some systems have software to identify false alarms.
- Most systems are small, low power devices that hang on trees or a placed elsewhere in a forest
- Satellite imagery can’t detect smoke from small fires

You can see from my research and the Pie chart above that forest fires was one of the top causes of deforestation. Based on this research finding, I’ve chosen forest fires as the risk that I will measure for my project.
From my research of existing systems, I’ve found that Most systems focus on Temperature, Soil Moisture, Flame and VOC sensors (smoke)”, therefore, I’ve chosen to use Temperature, Soil Moisture, Flame as my main variables and sensors. I didn’t use VOC because those sensors are harder to find and need a 12V power supply.
